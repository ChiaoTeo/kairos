# Portfolio management boundary

Document type: target architecture.

Portfolio is the Strategy-instance-owned consolidated record and historical
projection. It records what the Strategy owns and how that record is valued
across all of its Accounts and Segments. It is not a decision maker, exchange
account feed, order executor, risk budget store, or capital-transfer service.

## Why this owner is required now

The current system has concrete Portfolio callers and an ownership gap:

- Strategy needs one consolidated holdings, NAV, PnL, and exposure record
  across its configured Accounts;
- Risk already carries a `portfolio_version`, but Execution currently fills it
  from an Account event sequence rather than a real Portfolio fact;
- Capital needs group-level composition and liquidity context without becoming
  the owner of positions or valuation;
- backtest equity history currently lives in `StrategyApplication`, making the
  Strategy runtime a second owner of derived portfolio state.

`AccountApplication.snapshot()` correctly returns one internally consistent
snapshot per Account, but it does not create a cross-Account portfolio fact.
Expanding Account into a strategy-specific aggregator would mix external
account truth with Strategy ownership and valuation.

## Ownership

| Concern | Owner |
|---|---|
| Participant balances, positions, orders, equity, and freshness per Account | Account |
| Versioned consolidated holdings, cash, NAV, PnL, exposures, attribution, and history for one Strategy instance | Portfolio |
| Target position or desired portfolio decision | Strategy |
| Risk limits, budgets, admission decisions, stress policy, and reservations | Risk |
| Physical funding placement and Earn operations | Capital, when enabled |
| Exchange-facing order lifecycle | Execution |

Portfolio consumes immutable Account and Market facts and owns only the
derived consolidated record. It never approves an action or changes a target,
budget, order, or physical balance. It must preserve source evidence rather
than pretend several Account Actors share one atomic watermark.

## Runtime scope

Every trading Strategy instance constructs one in-process Portfolio runtime in
`kairospy`. It covers the same exclusive Account set as that Strategy instance
and is never shared between strategies. It is a lifecycle-owned application,
not a separate OS process. A signal-only launch with no Account and no
Execution may omit it explicitly.

Capital and Portfolio have different optionality:

```text
Account(s) + Market --> Portfolio --> Strategy
       |          |           |
       +----------+---------> Risk (independent enforcement)
                              |
                              +----> Capital (optional context)

Strategy --> Execution --> Risk --> participant order
Capital (optional) ----------> participant funding operation
```

- Portfolio is required for a trading launch because multi-Account exposure
  and valuation must have one owner.
- Risk remains a separate launch-scoped enforcement Actor. It consumes
  authoritative Account/Market facts and may use Portfolio projection evidence,
  but never trusts Strategy-supplied aggregate risk numbers as authorization.
- Capital is optional because a pre-funded Strategy can trade without
  automated funding movement.
- Capital failure must not block orders that Portfolio, Account, and Risk prove
  are already funded.

## Identity and consistency

`portfolio_id` is bound one-to-one to the Strategy launch identity and remains
stable across process restart. `portfolio_version` is generated by the
Portfolio Actor after a successful projection update; it is not copied from an
arbitrary Account sequence.

Every Portfolio snapshot records:

- the complete member Account set;
- an Account watermark vector of `account_id -> generation/event_sequence`;
- the Market/reference valuation watermark;
- completeness and freshness for every required Segment;
- the resulting `portfolio_version` and observation time.

There is no false cross-Account atomicity. If a required Account is missing,
stale, or ahead of an unresolved event gap, Portfolio is `Incomplete` or
`Stale`; Risk cannot combine that view with newer reservations as if it were
current.

## Minimal application surface

The first implementation needs queries and events, not a generalized manager:

```text
PortfolioSnapshot
  portfolio_id
  portfolio_version
  account_watermarks
  valuation_watermark
  cash_by_asset
  holdings_by_instrument
  exposure_by_asset/instrument/venue
  nav + realized_pnl + unrealized_pnl
  freshness + completeness

PortfolioUpdated
PortfolioBecameStale
PortfolioRecovered
```

Strategy may attach decision/target references for later attribution, but
Portfolio neither owns nor evaluates those targets and does not create orders.
Execution events do not directly mutate holdings: live holdings still enter
authoritatively through Account.

## Lifecycle

For a trading launch, composition initializes dependencies in this order:

```text
Account runtimes + Market/Reference
                -> in-process Portfolio rebuild/current
                -> Risk and Execution ready
                -> Strategy ready
                -> Capital ready if enabled
```

Capital readiness is not a Strategy trading prerequisite. Portfolio readiness
is. On shutdown, new Strategy decisions stop first, Execution commitments are
drained or reconciled, Portfolio persists its final projection, and Account
leases are released last.

## First implementation boundary

Implement the first vertical slice under
`kairospy/application/portfolio/`, with domain values and invariants kept in
that package and concrete projection/persistence wiring selected by Strategy
composition. Do not add `crates/modules/portfolio` or a
`kairos-portfolio-server` merely for symmetry.

Portfolio rebuilds from `AccountApplication.snapshot()` and consumes Account
and Market events before user Strategy callbacks observe the corresponding
portfolio change. `StrategyContext` exposes `portfolio`; it does not own the
projection. Backtest equity-curve production moves from `StrategyApplication`
to this owner.

When Rust Risk, Execution, or a later Capital process needs Portfolio facts,
Portfolio publishes a typed FlatBuffers snapshot/event carrying
`portfolio_version` and the full source-watermark vector. That cross-process
contract belongs under the Portfolio schema namespace; it must not be a JSON
dump or an application-owned trait mirror. Risk validates the referenced
Account/Market watermarks and performs its own risk-policy calculation; a
Portfolio projection is evidence, not permission. A separate Rust Portfolio
runtime is justified only by measured throughput, independent deployment, or
a concrete Rust-only state owner requirement.
